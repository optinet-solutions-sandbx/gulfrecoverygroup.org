What to do when something seems wrong

What to do as soon as a worrying sign appears.

Awareness guide

It might start with an unusual message, a phone call, a request to transfer funds, or even noticing a financial transaction you don't recall making. At that moment, doubt begins, and the most important question becomes: what should I do now?

In many cases, the first action isn't just the most important step — it can also be the factor that helps limit risk and organise information correctly.

When someone suspects financial or investment fraud, they may feel confused, anxious, or afraid of making the wrong decision. This can push them to act hastily, delete important information, or contact unknown parties, which may make the situation more complicated.

For this reason, dealing with suspicion calmly and methodically is an essential step.

In this guide, we will look at the most important steps recommended when you suspect financial fraud, how to protect your data, organise information, avoid common mistakes, and offer practical tips to help you deal with the situation with greater confidence.

When should you start to suspect something is wrong?

You don't need to have suffered a financial loss before you start asking questions.

Early warning signs may appear that are worth pausing and reassessing, such as:

  • A request to transfer additional funds without adequate explanation.
  • Messages or calls requesting personal or financial information.
  • Difficulty getting clear answers.
  • A sudden change in the way communication takes place.
  • A request to carry out actions urgently, without giving you enough time to think.
  • Unusual activity appearing in your accounts or digital wallets.

The presence of one of these indicators doesn't necessarily mean fraud is taking place, but it may be reason enough to pause temporarily and verify the information before taking any further step.

Why is staying calm important?

It's natural to feel anxious when you suspect a financial problem, but making decisions under the influence of fear can lead to further mistakes.

Some people delete messages, respond to new pressure, or share additional information in the hope of resolving the problem quickly.

By contrast, staying calm helps you:

  • Assess the situation objectively.
  • Review all the information available.
  • Arrange events in chronological order.
  • Preserve important evidence.
  • Choose the next step based on clear information.

Don't make hasty decisions

If you start to feel suspicious, it's best to give yourself a short period of time to review the situation.

Stop carrying out any new financial transfers until you have confirmed every detail, and don't share any additional information before understanding the reason for the request and who is making it.

Acting hastily can make it harder to review what happened later, whereas pausing gives you the chance to think calmly and organise your information.

What is the purpose of this guide?

This guide isn't intended to cause alarm — it aims to help you deal with any case of suspicion in an organised way, through practical steps that help protect your data, reduce mistakes, and strengthen your ability to make the right decision.

The first steps after suspecting fraud

When you sense that something isn't right, the first thing you should do is stop and avoid acting hastily. In many cases, the greatest danger isn't what has already happened, but the hasty decisions that may be made after the moment of suspicion.

Handling the situation in an organised way therefore helps protect your information and gives you the opportunity to understand what is happening before taking any action.

First: Stop any new transfers

If you're planning to transfer an additional amount or carry out a new financial transaction, it's best to postpone it until you've confirmed all the information.

It may help to ask yourself:

  • Do I understand the reason for this request?
  • Do I have all the information I need?
  • Is something pushing me to hurry?

If the answer isn't clear, it's best to pause temporarily and avoid taking any new financial step until the picture becomes clear.

Second: Don't share any additional information

If you start to suspect there's a problem, avoid sharing:

  • Your password.
  • Your verification code (OTP).
  • Your bank card details.
  • Your digital wallet recovery phrase (seed phrase).
  • Copies of your ID or personal documents, unless you're dealing with an official entity and there is a clear need.

Even if the request seems convincing, it's best to verify the identity of the party involved before sharing any sensitive information.

Third: Keep all the evidence

One of the most common mistakes people make is deleting messages or conversations immediately.

Instead, keep everything related to the case, such as:

  • Emails.
  • Conversations.
  • Transfer reference numbers.
  • Receipts.
  • Contracts.
  • Screenshots.
  • Links that were sent to you.
  • Names of the people or entities you communicated with.

Organising this information helps you review the case more effectively later on.

Fourth: Review your financial accounts

If the suspicion is related to a bank account, digital wallet, or investment platform, it's important to review:

  • Your most recent financial transactions.
  • Your login history, if available.
  • The devices linked to your account.
  • Any changes to your personal details.
  • Recent notifications.

If you notice any unusual activity, document it and keep all the details.

Fifth: Change your passwords if necessary

If you believe your login details may be at risk, it's best to change the passwords for your important accounts, particularly:

  • Email.
  • Online banking.
  • The investment platform.
  • The digital wallet.
  • Any service linked to these accounts.

It's advisable to use new, strong passwords and avoid reusing old ones.

Sixth: Use official channels only

If you need to contact a bank, platform, or service provider, always use:

  • The official website.
  • The official app.
  • Contact numbers published in official sources.

Don't rely on numbers or links you receive in unexpected messages before verifying them.

Seventh: Write down the timeline of events

It helps to write down what happened in chronological order, such as:

  • When did contact begin?
  • How did the contact happen?
  • What was requested of you?
  • When was any transaction carried out?
  • What messages or calls did you receive?

Having a clear timeline helps you understand the full picture and makes reviewing the case easier.

Common mistakes after suspecting something is wrong

Mistakes best avoided include:

  • Deleting messages or conversations.
  • Transferring additional funds without verification.
  • Sharing new information in an attempt to resolve the problem quickly.
  • Relying on unofficial sources for instructions.
  • Making decisions under the influence of anxiety or pressure.

Always remember

Suspicion doesn't necessarily mean fraud is taking place, but it is a signal urging you to stop, gather information, and verify before making any new decision.

Calm, organised action helps reduce mistakes and preserves important information you may need later.

How do you tell the difference between a technical issue and an attempted fraud?

When you encounter a problem with your bank account, an investment platform, or a digital wallet, it can be hard to know whether it's the result of a technical glitch or whether it calls for greater caution.

In some cases the problem is minor, such as a temporary outage or a system update, while in others indicators may appear that warrant deeper investigation.

For this reason, it is important to assess the situation calmly and avoid jumping to conclusions.

When is the problem technical?

You may encounter situations such as:

  • Delayed login.
  • Slowness on the website or app.
  • Temporary downtime of certain services.
  • A delay in a transaction appearing.
  • Scheduled system maintenance.

In such cases, many organisations announce maintenance work or outages through their official channels, so it's always advisable to check the official website or app before assuming there's a bigger problem.

When does something warrant further verification?

There are situations that call for greater attention, such as:

  • Receiving unusual requests to transfer additional funds.
  • Being asked to share passwords or verification codes.
  • A sudden change in the way communication takes place.
  • Inconsistent information provided by different staff members.
  • A request to carry out actions urgently without adequate explanation.
  • Transactions or notifications appearing that you don't recall making.

The presence of such indicators doesn't necessarily mean fraud is involved, but it warrants stopping and verifying before proceeding.

How do you organise evidence properly?

If you start to feel that something isn't right, organising your information may be one of the most important steps.

It's best to create a single file containing:

Details of the entity

  • The company name.
  • The website.
  • The email address.
  • Phone numbers.
  • Names of the people you dealt with.

Conversations

Keep copies of:

  • Emails.
  • WhatsApp conversations.
  • Telegram conversations.
  • Text messages.
  • Any other correspondence.

It's best not to edit or delete messages.

Financial transactions

Create a list including:

  • The date of each transaction.
  • The transaction amount.
  • The payment method.
  • The transaction reference, if any.
  • The receipt or confirmation.

Having an organised record makes it easier to review all the events later.

Documents

Keep a copy of:

  • Contracts.
  • Account statements.
  • Invoices.
  • Screenshots.
  • Any files sent or received.

Don't rely on memory

As time passes, it can become difficult to remember the exact order of events.

It's therefore best to write a simple timeline, such as:

  • When did contact begin?
  • When was the account created?
  • When was the first transaction made?
  • When did the problem appear?
  • What steps did you take afterwards?

This timeline helps you understand the full picture in an organised way.

How do you deal with pressure?

In some cases, the other party may try to convince you that you need to act quickly.

You may hear phrases such as:

  • If you don't complete this now, you'll lose the opportunity.
  • The process must be finished today.
  • You only have a few minutes.
  • Don't tell anyone until the process is complete.

In such cases, it's best to pause for a moment and reassess the situation.

Important financial decisions shouldn't be made under pressure or fear.

When should you pause communication temporarily?

If the information becomes unclear, if you start receiving repeated requests for sensitive data, or if you feel there are inconsistencies in the answers, it may be appropriate to pause communication temporarily so you can review all the information calmly.

Pausing communication doesn't mean taking a final stance — it gives you the time needed to confirm the facts before proceeding.

Quick checklist

If you start to feel suspicious, ask yourself:

  • Do I understand what is being asked of me?
  • Have I received a clear explanation?
  • Have I been given enough time to decide?
  • Am I keeping all the documents?
  • Have I checked the information against an official source?

If the answer is "no" to more than one question, it's best to pause temporarily until the picture becomes clear.

Always remember

The purpose of verification isn't to stop investing or halt financial dealings, but to help you make decisions based on clear information and reduce the likelihood of falling into mistakes that can be avoided through awareness and organisation.

What to do in the first 24 hours after suspecting something is wrong

The first hours after suspecting a financial problem or attempted fraud are among the most critical. The decisions made during this stage can help protect your data, organise your information, and reduce the likelihood of further mistakes.

Suspicion doesn't necessarily mean fraud is taking place, but it's reason enough to handle the situation cautiously until the picture becomes clear.

First: Stop any new financial steps

If you're about to make a new transfer or send additional documents, it's best to pause temporarily until you've reviewed all the information.

Avoid making any decision under pressure or in a hurry, and give yourself enough time to understand what is happening.

Second: Review all communication channels

Try to review all the messages you've received, whether through:

  • Email.
  • WhatsApp.
  • Telegram.
  • Text messages.
  • Phone calls.

Pay attention to:

  • The timing of the messages.
  • The nature of the requests.
  • Any changes in the style of communication.
  • Any new information requested of you.

This review may help you understand the sequence of events better.

Third: Check your devices

If you use a phone or computer to manage your financial accounts, it's a good idea to carry out a quick review.

Make sure to check:

  • That your operating system is up to date.
  • That your banking or investment apps are up to date.
  • Your list of installed apps.
