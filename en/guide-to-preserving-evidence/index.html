Guide to preserving evidence

How to properly document your transactions and conversations.

Awareness guide

Introduction

When dealing with any financial, investment, or digital service, keeping records of information and documents can become extremely important.

Contracts, receipts, correspondence, transfer records, and conversations all form part of the complete picture of any transaction.

Preserving evidence is not limited to cases where a problem arises; it is also one of the best practices that helps organise information and allows you to refer back to it whenever needed.

Why is preserving evidence important?

A person may need to review the following at any time:

  • A contract.
  • An invoice.
  • A payment receipt.
  • An email.
  • A conversation.
  • A transfer record.
  • Or any document related to the transaction.

The better organised and preserved this information is, the easier it becomes to access and review it.

What is meant by digital evidence?

Digital evidence is any electronic information that helps document what happened during a dealing with a service or transaction, such as:

  • Emails.
  • WhatsApp or Telegram conversations.
  • Screenshots.
  • Payment receipts.
  • Account statements.
  • Transaction IDs.
  • Contracts and agreements.
  • Official documents.
  • Recordings or notifications issued by official services, if keeping them is permitted under local laws.

What will you learn in this guide?

In this article, we will look at:

  • What evidence is best to keep.
  • How to organise files in a professional way.
  • The best ways to save conversations.
  • How to document payment and transfer transactions.
  • And how to create a well-organised file that is easy to refer back to when needed.

When should you start preserving evidence?

The answer is simple: from day one.

Don't wait until a problem appears.

Every message, document, or receipt may become an important reference later.

For this reason, ongoing documentation is far better than trying to gather information after a long period has passed.

Basic principles

When saving any piece of evidence, make sure to:

  • Keep the original copy if possible.
  • Do not alter the content of the document.
  • Organise files by date.
  • Name files clearly.
  • Create a backup copy in a safe place.

These steps make the review process easier and more professional.

What evidence should you keep?

When dealing with any financial, investment, or digital service, it is best to keep all information that documents what has happened since the relationship began.

Don't wait until a problem appears, as every document or message may later help you understand the timeline of the transaction.

First: Contracts and agreements

Keep a copy of all the documents you agreed to, such as:

  • Contracts.
  • Terms of use agreements.
  • The terms and conditions that were in effect at the time of registration.
  • Any amendments or updates sent afterwards.

It is preferable to keep the original copy in PDF format if available.

Second: Proof of payment

Every payment must be documented.

Keep:

  • Bank transfer receipts.
  • Bank card receipts.
  • Payment notifications.
  • Invoices.
  • Account statements.
  • Reference transaction numbers.

It is best to name files with the transaction date to make it easier to refer back to them.

Third: Cryptocurrency transactions

If the transaction was carried out using cryptocurrency, it is best to keep:

  • The transaction ID (TXID).
  • The sending wallet address.
  • The receiving wallet address.
  • The name of the network used.
  • The type of cryptocurrency.
  • The date and time of the transfer.
  • The transfer amount.

This information helps document the transaction accurately.

Fourth: Conversations

Save all important correspondence, whether it takes place via:

  • WhatsApp.
  • Telegram.
  • Email.
  • In-platform messages.
  • Any other official means of communication.

It is best for the conversations to be complete, showing:

  • The date.
  • The time.
  • The other party's name, if possible.
  • The sequence of messages without deletion or alteration.

Fifth: Screenshots

Screenshots can be useful for documenting:

  • The account status.
  • The displayed balance.
  • The transaction history.
  • The fees shown.
  • Notifications.
  • Important messages.

It is best to capture the full screen so that the date or time is visible whenever possible.

Sixth: Files and documents

Create a single folder that includes:

  • Contracts.
  • Receipts.
  • Copies of the identity documents you used during registration (if they were part of the transaction).
  • PDF files.
  • Invoices.
  • Any official documents.

Arranging files within a single folder makes them easier to access later.

Seventh: Create a timeline

One of the best ways to organise evidence is to create a simple file that includes:

DateEvent
01/05/2026Account opened
03/05/2026First deposit made
05/05/2026Confirmation message received
12/05/2026Withdrawal executed
15/05/2026New notification received

Having a clear timeline helps you understand events quickly, especially as the number of documents grows.

How do you organise evidence professionally?

Files can be divided into folders such as:

  • 📁 Contracts
  • 📁 Proof of payment
  • 📁 Bank transfers
  • 📁 Cryptocurrency transactions
  • 📁 Conversations
  • 📁 Email
  • 📁 Screenshots
  • 📁 Identity documents
  • 📁 Official correspondence

This organisation makes accessing any piece of information faster and easier.

Checklist for preserving evidence

Before finalising any transaction, make sure you have:

  • Saved the contract or agreement.
  • Kept proof of payment.
  • Saved all important conversations.
  • Kept screenshots when needed.
  • Recorded cryptocurrency transfer details (if any).
  • Organised all files within a single folder.
  • Updated the timeline of events.

Always remember

The best time to preserve evidence is before you need it.

Every message, receipt, document, and transaction record that is saved and organised from the outset makes reviewing any case in the future easier and more professional.

How do you preserve evidence in a way that prevents it from being lost?

Collecting evidence is an important step, but organising and storing it correctly is no less important.

In many cases, the problem is not a lack of evidence, but rather that it is scattered across the phone, email, and various applications, which makes it difficult to refer back to.

For this reason, creating a simple, organised system for preserving evidence helps you access any piece of information within minutes.

First: Keep the original copies

Whenever possible, keep files in their original format.

For example:

  • PDF files.
  • Original emails.
  • Bank account statements.
  • Transfer receipts.
  • CSV or Excel files when available.
  • Official reports.

Avoid altering these files or re-saving them in different formats unless there is a need to do so.

Second: Don't rely on your phone alone

Some people lose all their evidence when:

  • Changing phones.
  • Losing the device.
  • Performing a factory reset.
  • Deleting applications.

Therefore, it is best to keep an additional copy in a safe place, such as:

  • A personal computer.
  • An external storage drive.
  • A trusted cloud storage service.

Having more than one copy reduces the risk of losing information.

Third: Use clear file names

Instead of names such as:

  • Screenshot1
  • IMG0023
  • File.pdf

Use names that describe the content, such as:

  • Bank transfer - 12 May 2026
  • Payment receipt - credit card
  • WhatsApp conversation - customer service
  • Account statement - June 2026
  • Service contract - signed copy

This makes it easier to find the file later without having to open dozens of files.

Fourth: Arrange files by date

Use a clear chronological order. For example:

  • 📁 01 - Registration
  • 📁 02 - Identity verification
  • 📁 03 - Deposits
  • 📁 04 - Transfers
  • 📁 05 - Correspondence
  • 📁 06 - Withdrawal requests
  • 📁 07 - Notifications
  • 📁 08 - Final documents

The more logical the file arrangement, the easier it is to review.

Fifth: Create a document that summarises the story

Alongside the evidence, create a single document that includes:

  • The date the dealing began.
  • A brief description of each stage.
  • The important dates.
  • The amounts transferred.
  • The parties you contacted.
  • Any key events.

This summary saves considerable time when reviewing the file and helps you understand the timeline quickly.

Sixth: Don't delete any correspondence

Even messages that seem unimportant may later help in understanding the sequence of events.

Therefore, keep:

  • Text messages.
  • Emails.
  • Conversations.
  • Notifications.
  • Official replies.

It is best to save them in full, along with the date and time.

Seventh: Create regular backups

If you are constantly adding new documents, it is best to update the backup copy regularly.

The backup can be:

  • Weekly.
  • Monthly.
  • Or after every important transaction.

What matters is that it is kept in a location separate from the original copy.

What does an organised file look like?

It usually includes:

  • 📄 A timeline of events.
  • 📄 Contracts and agreements.
  • 📄 Proof of payment.
  • 📄 Account statements.
  • 📄 Cryptocurrency transactions (if any).
  • 📄 Conversations.
  • 📄 Emails.
  • 📄 Screenshots.
  • 📄 Official documents.

When all these elements are organised, accessing any piece of information becomes faster and easier.

Checklist for organising evidence

Before considering the file complete, make sure you have:

  • Kept the original copies.
  • Created a backup.
  • Named the files clearly.
  • Arranged them by date.
  • Prepared a timeline of events.
  • Kept all correspondence.
  • Placed all evidence within organised folders.

A golden rule

The value of a piece of evidence does not depend solely on its existence, but on how easily it can be found, understood, and linked to the rest of the documents.

How do you make sure your evidence file is complete?

After gathering and organising the documents, an equally important step follows: reviewing the entire file to make sure it contains all the essential information.

An organised file does not depend on the number of documents alone, but on whether anyone reviewing it can understand what happened from the very beginning through to the last action taken.

First: Review the timeline

Start by reading the events in order.

Make sure the file clearly shows:

  • When the relationship began.
  • When the account was created.
  • When the deposits were made.
  • When withdrawals were made (if any).
  • When the important correspondence began.
  • When any requests or changes appeared.

If there is a gap in time or an undocumented event, try to add what is missing.

Second: Make sure every transaction has proof

Every financial transaction should be matched by a document or piece of evidence, such as:

  • A payment receipt.
  • An account statement.
  • A transfer notification.
  • A transaction ID (TXID) in the case of cryptocurrency.
  • A confirmation message from the relevant party.

Having this link makes the file clearer and easier to review.

Third: Review the correspondence

Read the conversations from beginning to end.

Make sure that:

  • The messages are arranged by date.
  • There are no missing sections.
  • The date and time of the messages are visible.
  • The names of the parties are clear whenever possible.

A complete conversation gives a clearer picture than scattered screenshots.

Fourth: Check the official documents

Review all official files such as:

  • Contracts.
  • Invoices.
  • Policies.
  • Notifications.
  • Emails.

And make sure the saved copies are clear and legible.

Fifth: Review the file names

It is best for all files to have clear names, such as:

  • Service contract.pdf
  • Account statement - June 2026.pdf
  • Transfer receipt - 15 May.pdf
  • WhatsApp conversation - customer service.pdf

Clear names save considerable time during searching and review.

Sixth: Make sure a backup exists

After finishing organising the file:

  • Keep a copy on your device.
  • It is best to have an additional copy in a safe place.
  • Check that all files can be opened when needed.

The backup is an essential part of preserving evidence.
