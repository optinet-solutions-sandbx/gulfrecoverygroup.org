Guide to handling suspicious requests

What to do when you receive an unexplained financial request.

Awareness guide

Introduction

Some people, while using financial, investment, or digital services, receive financial requests they were not expecting.

These requests may relate to:

  • Additional fees.
  • Processing fees.
  • Withdrawal fees.
  • Verification fees.
  • Administrative fees.
  • Or amounts requested before a particular service is carried out.

The mere existence of a financial request does not mean it is invalid, but any unexpected request deserves a pause and verification before making any decision.

Knowing how to handle these requests helps you make calmer, more informed decisions.

Why should you verify before paying?

Before sending any amount, ask yourself:

  • Why am I being asked for this amount?
  • Is this request mentioned in the contract or terms?
  • Can I find this information on the official website?
  • Have I received a clear, written explanation?

The clearer the answers, the easier and safer the decision becomes.

What is meant by an unexpected financial request?

It is any request to pay an amount that the user did not expect when they began using the service, or was not previously aware of.

It may include, for example:

  • Processing fees.
  • Activation fees.
  • Verification fees.
  • Transfer fees.
  • Administrative fees.
  • A request for an additional deposit.
  • Or any new amount that was not previously discussed.

The existence of such a request does not necessarily mean there is a problem, but it deserves review and verification.

What will you learn in this guide?

In this article we will look at:

  • How to assess any new financial request.
  • What questions should be asked before paying.
  • How to verify fees.
  • When it is appropriate to ask for further clarification.
  • And how to make a decision based on information, not urgency.

The basic rule

Whenever a new financial request appears, do not make your first question "How much should I pay?" Instead, make your first question: "Why is this amount being requested, and where can I verify this information?"

This simple shift in thinking helps you assess any request more objectively.

Why do some people fall into this trap?

In some cases, a person may feel compelled to pay quickly because they:

  • Want to complete the transaction.
  • Fear wasting time.
  • Do not want to delay the process.
  • Believe this is the final step.

But pausing to review before paying gives you the chance to understand the details and make sure all the information is clear.

The most common types of financial requests that deserve verification

While using certain financial, investment, or digital services, a user may receive new requests to pay additional amounts.

The existence of such a request does not necessarily mean it is invalid, but it is important to understand the reason for the request, the basis on which it rests, and whether all the details are clearly explained.

First: a fee requested before a withdrawal is processed

Some users may receive a request to pay a fee before a withdrawal is processed.

Before making any decision, ask:

  • Is this fee mentioned in the contract or terms?
  • Is there an official page explaining it?
  • Have I received a written explanation of its reason?
  • Was I informed of it from the beginning of the relationship?

If the information is not clear, it is appropriate to request further clarification before paying.

Second: a request to deposit an additional amount

In some cases, a user may be asked to deposit a new amount before a particular service or procedure can be completed.

Before making any new transfer, try to find out:

  • Why is this amount being requested?
  • Is there a document explaining the reason?
  • Is this step mentioned in the agreement?
  • Are there alternatives or other options?

Every new request deserves review, even if you have dealt with the same entity before.

Third: a request for verification or compliance fees

Some services may include verification or regulatory compliance procedures, but it is important that all such procedures be explained and documented in advance.

Before paying any fee related to verification, ask:

  • What is the purpose of this fee?
  • Is it mentioned in the terms?
  • Where can I read its details?
  • Is it issued by the same entity or by another party?

Clarity and transparency are essential elements in any financial transaction.

Fourth: a request for administrative or operational fees

A request relating to administrative fees or operating fees may appear.

Before agreeing:

  • Ask for a written explanation.
  • Review the agreement.
  • Confirm the fee amount and how it is calculated.
  • Check that it appears in the official fee schedule.

The more documented the information, the easier it is to assess.

Fifth: a request to transfer funds to a different account

If you are asked to transfer funds to:

  • A new account.
  • A different IBAN number.
  • A new digital wallet.
  • A payment method not previously used.

It is best to pause and verify the reason for this change through official channels before carrying out any transfer.

Sixth: requests accompanied by urgency

Some requests may include phrases such as:

  • Payment must be made today.
  • You have only a few hours.
  • If you delay, you will lose the opportunity.
  • The procedure must be completed immediately.

In financial transactions, urgency should never stop you from reviewing information or asking questions.

If you feel time pressure, it is best to give yourself time to understand all the details.

Seventh: requests that do not include clear documentation

If you receive a financial request without:

  • An invoice.
  • A contract.
  • An official notice.
  • A written explanation.
  • A clear reference.

Then ask for official documentation before taking any step.

Documenting information helps you understand and review the request more effectively.

Checklist before paying any new amount

Before sending any amount, make sure you have:

  • Understood the reason for the request.
  • Read all documents related to it.
  • Reviewed the terms and conditions.
  • Verified the payment method.
  • Received a clear, written explanation.
  • Not made the decision under time pressure.
  • Kept all correspondence and documents.

How do you assess any financial request before deciding to pay?

When you receive a new financial request, do not let your first reaction be to pay or refuse — instead, begin a structured verification process.

Asking the right questions before transferring funds helps you understand the reason for the request and confirm that all details are clear and understood.

First: fully understand the reason for the request

Before any transfer, ask for a clear, written explanation.

Ask:

  • Why is this amount being requested?
  • What service or procedure does it cover?
  • Is there a document explaining this?
  • Is this request part of the original agreement?

If you cannot obtain a clear answer, it is best not to rush into a decision.

Second: review the contract and terms

Go back to:

  • The contract.
  • The terms and conditions.
  • The fee schedule.
  • The deposit and withdrawal policy.

And check whether the request is clearly mentioned in them.

If you find no reference to it, ask for official clarification before proceeding.

Third: request an official document

If you are asked to pay any amount, you have the right to request:

  • An invoice.
  • An official notice.
  • A statement explaining the reason for the fee.
  • A reference to the policy or clause the request is based on.

Written documentation helps you understand the request better and provides a reference you can return to later.

Fourth: verify the payment method

Before transferring any amount, confirm:

  • The beneficiary's name.
  • The payment method.
  • The bank account or wallet address.
  • The reason for using this method.

If you notice an unexpected change in payment details, it is best to verify the reason through official channels.

Fifth: do not rely on conversations alone

A request may be sent via:

  • WhatsApp.
  • Telegram.
  • Email.
  • A text message.

But it is best to also confirm that this request appears in:

  • Your official account.
  • The contract.
  • The website.
  • Or any official document issued by the entity.

The more official sources in which you find the information, the more accurately you can assess it.

Sixth: do not let urgency drive the decision

If you are asked to pay within minutes or hours, it is helpful to give yourself time to review.

Important financial decisions deserve to be made after reading and understanding all the details, not under time pressure.

Seventh: keep all correspondence

Create a file containing:

  • Emails.
  • Important conversations.
  • Invoices.
  • Receipts.
  • Contracts.
  • Screenshots.
  • Any documents related to the request.

Good documentation helps you review any request later and gives you a clearer picture of every stage.

Checklist of questions before paying any amount

Before carrying out any payment, ask:

  • Why is this amount being requested?
  • Is there an official document explaining the reason?
  • Is this request mentioned in the contract or terms?
  • Have I verified the beneficiary's details?
  • Have I reviewed all the fees?
  • Have I kept all the documents?
  • Did I make the decision after sufficient review?

What should you do if you feel pressured to pay a sum of money?

In some cases, a person may feel pressured to make a financial decision quickly, especially if told that delay could disrupt the service or cause them to lose a particular opportunity.

The presence of pressure does not necessarily mean the request is invalid, but it is reason enough to pause and review all the details before making a decision.

First: do not make the decision under pressure

If you feel that time does not allow you to review, it is best to ask for a short extension.

Use this time to:

  • Read the documents.
  • Review the fees.
  • Re-read the terms.
  • Ask any additional questions.
  • Confirm all the information.

A good financial decision does not depend on speed, but on clarity of information.

Second: request all details in writing

If you are asked to pay a new amount, it is best to request:

  • A written explanation.
  • The fee amount in detail.
  • The reason for the request.
  • The contractual reference or clause it is based on.
  • Any official document explaining the procedure.

Having information in writing helps you review it calmly and reduces the likelihood of misunderstanding.

Third: verify the entity requesting payment

Before carrying out any transfer, confirm:

  • The name of the entity.
  • The account or wallet details.
  • The official channels of communication.
  • That the request came through approved channels.

If the payment method or beneficiary details change unexpectedly, it is best to request confirmation through another official channel before proceeding.

Fourth: do not let fear drive the decision

Phrases such as these may be used:

  • If you do not pay today, the process will stop.
  • This is the last chance.
  • The account will be closed.
  • You will not be able to continue if you delay.

Instead of making an immediate decision, ask:

  • Is there a document explaining this?
  • Is this procedure specified in the agreement?
  • Can I verify this information from an official source?

The more a decision is based on facts, the safer it is.

Fifth: consult someone whose judgement you trust

If the amount is large or the details are unclear, it may help to review the information with an experienced person or a specialist adviser before paying.

A second opinion often helps you notice details that were not clear at first.

Sixth: keep all the evidence

Before making any decision, keep:

  • Emails.
  • Conversations.
  • Invoices.
  • Contracts.
  • Receipts.
  • Screenshots.
  • Any notices related to the request.

Organising these documents helps you review the situation better if you need to later.

Seventh: give yourself the right to pause

If you feel the information is incomplete or that some details are still unclear, you have the right to postpone payment until the full picture is clear.

A temporary pause for review does not mean refusing the request — it means making sure you are making a conscious, responsible decision.

Checklist before approving any financial request

Before sending any amount, make sure you have:

  • Fully understood the reason for the request.
  • Received a written explanation.
  • Reviewed the contract and terms.
  • Verified the beneficiary's details.
  • Kept all the documents.
  • Not made the decision under time pressure.
  • Reviewed the information through official channels.

A golden rule

If you need more time to understand any financial request, you have the right to ask for clarification before making a decision. Verifying before paying is part of responsible financial management.

Always remember

The best financial decisions are those made after reviewing all the information, reading the documents, and understanding the reasons — not the result of urgency or pressure.

The more time you give yourself to verify, the better able you are to protect your money and make decisions based on knowledge.

Conclusion

While using financial, investment, or digital services, you may encounter requests to pay fees or additional amounts that you did not expect at the start of the engagement.

The appearance of such requests does not necessarily mean there is a problem, but every new financial request deserves verification before any decision is made.

Understanding the reason for the request, reading the documents, reviewing the terms, and verifying the entity requesting payment are all steps that help you make a decision based on information, not on urgency or pressure.

The best way to protect your money is not to refuse every request, but to make sure you fully understand it before carrying it out.

  • Fully understood the reason for the request.
  • Read the contract and terms and conditions.
  • Reviewed all the fees.
  • Received a written clarification if needed.
  • Verified the beneficiary entity's details.
  • Kept all documents and correspondence.
  • Made the decision after sufficient review, not because of time pressure.

Mistakes to avoid

It is best to avoid:

  • Transferring funds before understanding the reason for the request.
  • Relying on verbal conversations alone.
  • Ignoring the terms and conditions.
  • Paying under time pressure.
  • Sending money to a different payment method without verifying the reason.
