Digital security

The essentials of protecting your devices, accounts, and data.

Awareness guide

Introduction

The internet has become an essential part of our daily lives, as we rely on it to manage bank accounts, communicate, work, shop, invest, and use digital wallets and government services.

With this development, protecting personal information, accounts, and devices has become a responsibility no less important than protecting money itself.

Digital security is not limited to using protection software; it also includes daily habits, the way you use the internet, and the ability to verify information before sharing any data.

What is meant by digital security?

Digital security is a set of practices that help protect:

  • Online accounts.
  • Personal data.
  • Devices.
  • Email.
  • Digital wallets.
  • Bank accounts.
  • Digital identity.

It aims to reduce the risks associated with using the internet and to preserve the user's privacy and information.

Why has digital security become important?

Today, a person can use their phone alone to:

  • Transfer money.
  • Manage bank accounts.
  • Use digital wallets.
  • Invest online.
  • Keep personal files.
  • Access work accounts.

For this reason, protecting your device and accounts has become an essential part of managing daily life.

Digital security does not depend on software alone

Some believe that installing protection software is enough.

In reality, however, digital security also depends on:

  • The strength of passwords.
  • Verifying links.
  • Updating devices.
  • Protecting email.
  • Not sharing sensitive information.
  • Using official channels.

In many cases, the user's behaviour is the first line of defence.

What will you learn in this article?

We will look at:

  • How to protect your accounts.
  • How to create strong passwords.
  • The importance of two-factor authentication.
  • How to protect your personal data.
  • How to deal with suspicious messages and links.
  • Best practices for keeping your devices secure.

Why does this guide focus on habits?

Because most digital security problems do not start because of technology, but because of a simple decision such as:

  • Clicking a link.
  • Using a weak password.
  • Sharing a verification code.
  • Installing an unknown app.
  • Using an unsecured network.

For this reason, building sound digital habits is one of the most important means of protection.

How do you protect your accounts and digital identity?

Most digital services today rely on online accounts, whether banking, email, digital wallets, investment platforms, or social media accounts.

For this reason, protecting these accounts has become one of the most important elements of digital security.

First: Use a strong, unique password

A password is the first line of defence for your accounts.

It should preferably be:

  • Long.
  • Hard to guess.
  • Different for each account.
  • A mix of letters, numbers, and symbols.

It is also advisable not to use personal information such as:

  • Your date of birth.
  • Your phone number.
  • The name of a family member.
  • Easy or repeated words.

Using a different password for each service reduces the risk if one account is compromised.

Second: Enable two-factor authentication (2FA)

Even if someone learns your password, two-factor authentication adds an extra layer of protection.

This system usually relies on:

  • An authentication app.
  • A temporary code.
  • A security key.
  • Additional verification methods provided by the service.

Enabling this feature helps protect accounts from unauthorised login attempts.

Third: Make your email the most protected of your accounts

Email is the key to most of your digital accounts.

Through it, you can:

  • Reset passwords.
  • Receive verification codes.
  • Manage various accounts.

For this reason, your email should be:

  • Protected by a strong password.
  • Set up with two-factor authentication.
  • Linked to up-to-date recovery methods.
  • Regularly checked for login activity.

Protecting your email means protecting a large part of your digital identity.

Fourth: Monitor login activity

Many services allow you to review:

  • Connected devices.
  • Login locations.
  • The most recent logins.
  • Trusted devices.

It is good practice to review this information from time to time, especially if you notice any unusual activity.

Fifth: Do not share verification codes

You may receive one-time verification codes (OTPs) via:

  • Text messages.
  • Email.
  • Authentication apps.

These codes are for you alone.

Do not share them with anyone, even if they claim to be a support employee or customer service representative.

In most official services, you are never asked to share a verification code with anyone.

Sixth: Update your recovery information

Make sure that:

  • Your phone number is correct.
  • Your backup email is up to date.
  • Your account recovery methods are working.

Having correct recovery information helps you regain access to your account if you ever need to.

Seventh: Use a password manager

If you use a large number of accounts, a password manager can help you:

  • Create strong passwords.
  • Store them securely.
  • Use a different password for each service.
  • Reduce reliance on memory.

This reduces the reuse of the same password across multiple accounts.

How do you know your account needs a review?

Review your security settings if you notice:

  • A login you do not recognise.
  • Verification messages you did not request.
  • Notifications of a password change you did not make.
  • New devices connected to the account.
  • Changes to your recovery information.

In these cases, it is best to review your security settings immediately.

Checklist for protecting your accounts

Before finishing the setup of any new account, make sure that:

  • You have used a strong password.
  • The password is different from your other accounts.
  • You have enabled two-factor authentication.
  • You have reviewed your recovery information.
  • You know which devices are connected to the account.
  • You have not shared any verification code with anyone.

How do you protect your personal data while using the internet?

Digital protection is not limited to passwords alone; it also includes how you use the internet, apps, devices, and various services.

In many cases, personal information may be collected or shared without the user noticing, which is why daily awareness is an essential part of digital security.

First: Share as little information as possible

Before entering any data, ask yourself:

  • Is this information necessary?
  • Is the party requesting it trustworthy?
  • Do I understand why this data is being requested?

The less information you share, the lower the likelihood of it being misused.

It is preferable not to share information such as:

  • ID numbers.
  • Photos of official documents.
  • Banking details.
  • Passwords.
  • Verification codes.
  • Digital wallet information.

Unless necessary, and only through official channels.

Second: Beware of phishing messages

Phishing messages are among the most commonly used methods in cybercrime.

They may reach you via:

  • Email.
  • Text messages.
  • WhatsApp.
  • Telegram.
  • Social media.

They may contain:

  • Login links.
  • A request to update your data.
  • Notifications of a problem with your account.
  • Offers or prizes.
  • Urgent requests to confirm your identity.

Before clicking any link:

  • Check the sender.
  • Review the website address.
  • Use the official website if possible.
  • Do not enter your data before confirming the source.

Third: Use secure networks

When using the internet in public places, try to avoid carrying out sensitive transactions such as:

  • Banking services.
  • Digital wallets.
  • Investment platforms.
  • Changing passwords.

If you must use a public network, make sure the connection is secure, and it is preferable to use a private network or an additional protection method when needed.

Fourth: Keep your devices continuously updated

Updating your phone or computer does not just add new features; it also includes:

  • Security fixes.
  • Patches for vulnerabilities.
  • Protection improvements.
  • System updates.

Therefore, regularly installing official updates helps strengthen your level of security.

Fifth: Download apps only from official sources

Before installing any app:

  • Use the device's official store.
  • Check the developer's name.
  • Read the app's description.
  • Review the permissions it requests.

Do not install apps from unknown links or unofficial websites, especially if they request broad permissions without justification.

Sixth: Review the permissions you grant to apps

Some apps request access to:

  • The camera.
  • The microphone.
  • Location.
  • Contacts.
  • Photos and files.

Before agreeing, ask yourself:

  • Does the app really need this permission?
  • Can it be used without it?

Regularly reviewing permissions helps protect your privacy.

Seventh: Do not store sensitive data in unsafe places

It is best not to store:

  • Passwords.
  • Verification codes.
  • Bank card details.
  • Digital wallet recovery phrases.

In unprotected notes, chat apps, or photos on your phone.

Use secure, dedicated storage methods if you need to keep this information.

The most common mistakes in digital security

Mistakes that can be avoided include:

  • Using the same password for all accounts.
  • Clicking links without verifying them.
  • Sharing verification codes.
  • Installing unknown apps.
  • Ignoring system updates.
  • Using public networks for financial transactions.
  • Not reviewing privacy and security settings.

Checklist for protecting your data

Before using any online service, make sure that:

  • You are using the official website or app.
  • You have checked the link before clicking it.
  • You do not share passwords or verification codes.
  • You are using a secure network.
  • You update your device regularly.
  • You have reviewed app permissions.
  • You store your sensitive information securely.

What should you do if you suspect a security problem?

Even when following the best digital security practices, a user may sometimes notice signs worth checking, such as unusual notifications or activity they did not carry out themselves.

The appearance of these signs does not necessarily mean the account has been hacked, but they deserve to be dealt with quickly and calmly.

First: Do not ignore security notifications

If you receive a notification relating to:

  • A login attempt.
  • A password change.
  • A new device being added.
  • A change to account information.
  • A password reset request.

and this action is unfamiliar to you, it is best to review your account immediately and confirm whether the activity is legitimate.

Second: Change your password immediately when needed

If you feel your account may be at risk:

  • Change your password immediately.
  • Use a new, strong password.
  • Do not use your previous password or similar words.
  • If you use the same password on other accounts, update those as well.

Third: Review connected devices

Most online services allow you to review the devices that have logged into the account.

Check:

  • Device names.
  • Approximate locations.
  • The date of the last login.
  • Active sessions.

If you notice a device you do not recognise, end the session and review your security settings.

Fourth: Enable or re-enable two-factor authentication

If you have not yet enabled two-factor authentication, now is the right time.

If it is already enabled, make sure that:

  • Your verification method is still under your control.
  • Your phone number or authentication app is working correctly.
  • Your recovery methods are up to date.

Fifth: Check your device

If you notice unusual behaviour on your phone or computer, such as:

  • Sudden slowness.
  • Apps you don't remember installing.
  • Strange windows or notifications.
  • Abnormal battery or data usage.

It may be worth:

  • Updating the system.
  • Removing unknown apps.
  • Running a security scan using trusted tools.
  • Reviewing app permissions again.

Sixth: Review linked accounts

Your email or phone number may be linked to several services.

So, if a problem occurs with one account, it is also useful to review:

  • Bank accounts.
  • Digital wallets.
  • Investment accounts.
  • Social media.
  • Cloud storage services.

To make sure there are no unrecognised changes.

Seventh: Keep evidence if you notice unusual activity

If you notice unfamiliar activity, it is best to keep:

  • Screenshots.
  • Emails.
  • Alert messages.
  • The login log.
  • The date and time of events.
  • Any reference numbers or identifiers related to the account.

Documenting this information helps you review what happened and contact the relevant authority if necessary.

How do you build long-term digital protection?

Make these habits part of your daily use:

  • Regularly review your security settings.
  • Update your passwords when needed.
  • Use two-factor authentication.
  • Keep your devices continuously updated.
  • Monitor your account activity.
